## LEGAL ACCURACY NOTES

- **DTSA 18 U.S.C. §§ 1836-1839** — correct citation. §1836 is the civil cause of action; §1839 is the definitional section. ✓
- **Copyright Act 17 U.S.C. §§ 101 et seq.** — correct umbrella citation. For an infringement claim specifically the operative sections are §501 (infringement) and §504 (damages). Recommend tightening to "§§ 101, 106, 501, 504" in the eventual complaint draft (the §I umbrella cite is fine for the memo).
- **Patent Act 35 U.S.C. §§ 271, 281, 284, 285** — correct: §271 (infringement), §281 (right of action), §284 (damages), §285 (attorney's fees). ✓
- **Lanham Act 15 U.S.C. § 1125(a)** — correct citation for false designation / false advertising. ✓
- **'34 Act § 10(b) / Rule 10b-5 / 15 U.S.C. § 78j(b) / 17 C.F.R. § 240.10b-5** — correct triple cite. ✓
- **Aronson v. Lewis, 473 A.2d 805 (Del. 1984)** — correct citation for Delaware demand-futility framework. Note: largely refined by *Brehm v. Eisner*, 746 A.2d 244 (Del. 2000) and substantially superseded by *United Food & Commercial Workers Union v. Zuckerberg*, 262 A.3d 1034 (Del. 2021), which is now the controlling demand-futility test. For a 2026-filed action **the operative cite should be `Zuckerberg`**, with `Aronson` as historical antecedent. Recommend updating before any complaint is filed. (For the memo, citing both as the doctrinal anchor is acceptable.)
- **Malpiede v. Townson, 780 A.2d 1075 (Del. 2001)** — correct citation for aiding-and-abetting breach of fiduciary duty under Delaware law. ✓
- **Delaware as governing for fiduciary-duty / aiding-and-abetting** — appropriate because (i) Lux Industries Inc. is a Delaware corporation per §II framing, and (ii) Delaware's internal-affairs doctrine controls fiduciary duties owed to a Delaware corporation regardless of where the breach was performed. ✓
- **California for fraud / unjust enrichment / conversion / tortious-interference / civil-conspiracy** — appropriate if Counterparty's principal place of business is California (§II Counterparty Entity Structure paragraph leaves this to outside-counsel diligence — confirm before filing). Note that civil conspiracy in California is **not an independent tort**; it requires an underlying tort and operates only as a vicarious-liability mechanism (`Applied Equipment Corp. v. Litton Saudi Arabia Ltd.`, 7 Cal.4th 503 (1994)). The memo's framing as a "count" is fine for memo purposes but the complaint should plead conspiracy as a basis for joint-and-several liability tied to the underlying torts, not as a standalone count.
- **Forum recommendation: NDCal + parallel Delaware Chancery** — coherent. Bifurcating federal IP + 10b-5 in NDCal from internal-affairs counts in Chancery is standard practice. ✓
- **Litigation hold paragraph** — appropriately specific (30-day window, adverse-inference instruction). ✓
- **Rule 10b-5 standing premise** — the memo's Count IX theory hangs on Lux being a purchaser/seller of Counterparty securities (Blue Chip Stamps standing rule). Confirm in §V that Lux's planned equity acquisition (or the prior round's allocation, if any) supplies Blue Chip standing — otherwise 10b-5 is unavailable to Lux directly and the fraud claim has to ride on Forum Markets or new-money investors as the deceived parties, with Lux's hook being something other than securities-fraud-as-purchaser.
- **Forum Markets Form 8-K disclosure flag** at §II lines 384-386 — correctly noted that Forum Markets' public-issuer status raises potential 8-K materiality on its own side of any decision to anchor a material investment in the Counterparty. This is a sound litigation-leverage observation.
